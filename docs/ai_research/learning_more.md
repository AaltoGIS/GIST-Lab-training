# Where to learn more

AI moves fast, and the practical way to keep up is to follow a few good sources
rather than chase everything. Here is an opinionated list, grouped by what you want
from it. It is a living list — tell the lab about anything you have found useful.

## Keep up with the news

- **[The AI Daily Brief](https://aidailybrief.ai/)** — a short daily podcast on what
  happened in AI and why it matters. Good for staying current without reading
  everything.
- **[One Useful Thing](https://www.oneusefulthing.org/)** — Ethan Mollick's newsletter
  on what AI means for how people actually work, learn, and write. The closest fit to a
  research audience.
- **[The Batch](https://www.deeplearning.ai/the-batch/)** — DeepLearning.AI's weekly
  newsletter: the developments that matter, explained plainly. A gentle way in.
- **[Import AI](https://importai.substack.com/)** — Jack Clark's weekly newsletter,
  closer to the research and policy edge. More technical.
- **[AI as Normal Technology](https://www.normaltech.ai/)** — Narayanan and Kapoor, the
  *AI Snake Oil* authors, on treating AI as capable but not magic. The useful skeptical
  read.

## Episodes worth an hour: The AI Daily Brief

A hand-picked set of episodes that map onto this theme, in the order the theme reads.
Each is 20–45 minutes. YouTube links where the video exists; a few are podcast-only.

**What AI and agents are for** — {doc}`What you can use AI and agents for <what_you_can_use_ai_for>`

- [How People Actually Use AI Agents](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WBN1CyBy8bE) —
  what an Anthropic study found about real agent use: short sessions, heavy human
  oversight, far more conservative than the capabilities allow.
- [Why Agents Still Need Humans](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GWPpLdpTo90) — the
  "human sandwich": automation creates more expert human work, not less.
- [Why AI Actually Won't Take Your Job](https://open.spotify.com/episode/4jzLPYH1R2j8eXROdDWYPl)
  (podcast) — tasks versus jobs, and why "AI-washed" layoffs mislead.
- [AI Inequality](https://open.spotify.com/episode/4nT8Lda6aj4RwpmcwecBNV) (podcast) —
  the divide between who gets the strongest models and who gets the weaker tiers — and
  why this theme recommends a capable model for work that matters.

**Getting started with coding agents** — {doc}`Getting started <getting_started>`,
{doc}`Higher-quality results <higher_quality_results>`

- [How to Start AI Coding If You Haven't Yet](https://podcasts.apple.com/us/podcast/how-to-start-ai-coding-if-you-havent-yet/id1680633614?i=1000786706158)

**Data control** — {doc}`Data protection and privacy <data_protection>`

  involves.

## Go deeper

- **[Machine Learning Street Talk (MLST)](https://www.youtube.com/c/machinelearningstreettalk)**
  — long-form interviews and discussions on the research and ideas behind AI, for when
  you want depth rather than headlines.
- **[Andrej Karpathy](https://www.youtube.com/@AndrejKarpathy)** — clear, hands-on video
  explainers of how large language models work, from a leading researcher. Start here to
  understand the machinery.
- **[Dwarkesh Podcast](https://www.dwarkesh.com/)** — long, well-prepared interviews with
  researchers and lab leaders.
- **[3Blue1Brown](https://www.3blue1brown.com/)** — visual explanations of the
  mathematics behind neural networks.
- **[Latent Space](https://www.latent.space/)** — "AI engineering": how teams actually
  build with agents, models, and tools.

## Learn Claude Code and the tools

- **Anthropic's documentation** — the
  [Claude Code docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/overview) for the agent and its
  features, and the [platform docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/home) for the
  Claude models. Start there for how-to questions.
- Anthropic's [courses](https://github.com/anthropics/courses),
  [cookbook](https://github.com/anthropics/claude-cookbooks), and
  [YouTube channel](https://www.youtube.com/@anthropic-ai) — worked examples and
  walkthroughs, including prompting.
- **[Building effective agents](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/building-effective-agents)**
  — Anthropic's short guide to building agents from simple, composable parts. Pairs with
  this theme's material on agents.
- **[DeepLearning.AI short courses](https://www.deeplearning.ai/short-courses/)** — free,
  hands-on courses on agents, tools, and building with models.
- **[Simon Willison's blog](https://simonwillison.net/)** — frequent, practical notes on
  LLMs and the tooling around them; strong for research-software work. See his guide to
  [using git with coding agents](https://simonwillison.net/guides/agentic-engineering-patterns/using-git-with-coding-agents/).

## Responsible and efficient AI in research

- **[Aalto's best practices for AI use in research work](https://www.aalto.fi/en/research-art/best-practices-for-ai-use-in-research-work)**
  — your own institution's practical guide to AI across the research lifecycle. Start
  local.
- **[ERA living guidelines on generative AI in research](https://research-and-innovation.ec.europa.eu/research-area/industrial-research-and-innovation/artificial-intelligence-ai-science_en)**
  — the EU's evolving recommendations for researchers, organisations, and funders. Also
  linked from {doc}`Integrity, disclosure, and provenance <integrity_and_disclosure>`.
- **[The Turing Way](https://book.the-turing-way.org/)** — an open handbook on
  reproducible, ethical, collaborative research. A companion to the
  {doc}`Reproducibility and record-keeping <reproducibility>` page.
- **[Co-Intelligence](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/741805/co-intelligence-by-ethan-mollick/)**
  — Ethan Mollick's short book on working alongside AI, if you want a single accessible
  read.
