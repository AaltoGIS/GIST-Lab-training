# Integrity, disclosure, and provenance

Using AI does not change what research integrity asks of you. Honesty about what
you did, and accountability for the result, apply the same whether a paragraph
came from your head, a colleague, or a model. This page covers the parts people
most often get wrong: authorship, fabrication, disclosure, and where your material
came from.

## AI is not an author

:::{important}
An AI tool is never an author. Authorship means taking responsibility for the work
and being able to answer for it — a tool can do neither. You are the author; the
model is something you used.
:::

This is the settled position of journals, publishers, and research-integrity
bodies. Record AI use where you would describe any other tool or method, not in the
author line.

## Don't pass off what you haven't checked

Models produce fluent references, quotes, numbers, and results that look right and
are sometimes invented. Presenting any of them as verified when you have not
checked them is fabrication — and fabrication is misconduct regardless of whether a
person or a model produced it.

**Do**

- Open and read every citation before it stays in your work.
- Reproduce any AI-suggested number or result yourself before you report it.

**Don't**

- Paste a reference list a model generated without confirming each entry exists.
- Report a figure or statistic you cannot regenerate.

## Disclose how you used it

Be transparent about AI's role. Three practices, used together:

- **A line in the methods or acknowledgements** naming the tools and what you used
  them for — drafting, code assistance, language editing. For example: *"We used
  Claude (Anthropic) to draft and edit analysis code and to improve the English of
  this manuscript. All output was reviewed and verified by the authors, who take full
  responsibility for the content."*
- **A record in the project itself** — a note in the repository, or an
  ``AI assistants:`` line in a pull request or commit listing the tools and
  settings used. Keeping this record is part of
  {doc}`Reproducibility and record-keeping <reproducibility>`.
- **The venue's own policy.** Journals, publishers, and funders each set their own
  disclosure rules, and they change. Check the current one for where you are
  submitting.

## Know where your material came from

AI can generate or transform code, text, data, and images, and what comes out may
carry someone else's rights with it.

Before you reuse generated material, check its **provenance**: who owns it, what
license applies, whether attribution is required, and where the underlying content
came from. A model can reproduce licensed code or copyrighted text without telling
you. You are responsible for what you incorporate, so keep track of the source.

One way to keep provenance clear is to supply the sources yourself. Some tools work
only from material you give them — your Zotero library through Beaver, or your own
PDFs and notes in a notebook tool such as Gemini Notebook — and cite each point back
to that material instead of to sources you cannot see. Because you chose what went in,
you already know where the output came from. See
{doc}`Useful companion tools <companion_tools>` for both.

## Where to check the rules

The specifics differ by institution, funder, and venue, and they are moving
quickly. Consult the current version of:

- Aalto's [research-integrity](https://www.aalto.fi/en/services/research-integrity)
  and [responsible-use-of-AI-in-research](https://www.aalto.fi/en/services/responsible-use-of-generative-artificial-intelligence-in-the-research-process)
  guidance.
- [TENK](https://tenk.fi/en), the Finnish National Board on Research Integrity — its
  national recommendation
  [Artificial Intelligence in Research](https://tenk.fi/en/news/tenk-publishes-new-national-recommendation-ai-research).
- Your funder's stance — the
  [Research Council of Finland](https://www.aka.fi/en/research-funding/apply-for-funding/how-to-apply-for-funding/az-index-of-application-guidelines/artificial-intelligence/),
  or [ERC / Horizon Europe](https://research-and-innovation.ec.europa.eu/research-area/industrial-research-and-innovation/artificial-intelligence-ai-science_en).
- The target journal's or publisher's author policy.
- [COPE, on authorship and AI](https://publicationethics.org/guidance/cope-position/authorship-and-ai-tools).

When sources differ, meet the stricter one — and remember a venue's permission never
overrides the law or your institution's rules.
